*(The large parish hall of the presbytery. A large dark wooden table is cluttered with pious images, candles and factory registers. The Priest carefully folds chasubles and puts away his missals. The door swings open. The Midwife enters, a large wicker basket under her arm, overflowing with linen sheets and dried herbs, followed by the Garde Champêtre who carries two large ones at arm's length wooden tubs and rolls of tow strips.)*

**The Priest:** *(surprised by the maneuver, interposing in front of the table)*
What does this noise mean, Mademoiselle? You are not at the market!

**The Midwife:**
Put that on the benches, Guard.

**The Priest:** 
These benches are necessary for me for catechism.

**The Midwife:**
The catechism will wait until the lungs of the canton stop wheezing! Guard, you can leave. Thanks for your help!

**The Country Guard:** *(wiping the sweat from his forehead with his sleeve, hesitating)*
With all my respect, Miss...
*(He glances worriedly at the Priest, lowers his head and rushes out.)*

**The Priest:**
It’s sacrilege, Mademoiselle! This room is reserved for the works of the Factory and the comfort of souls! You cannot transform the presbytery into an infirmary without the agreement of the bishopric!

**The Midwife:** *(roughly unloading her bundles of linen on the table, pushing the candles aside)*
Your souls, Mr. Priest, will have plenty of time to burn in purgatory if their bodies suffocate in their spittle by the end of the week! Hot water and cresyl, that’s what you need! Not paternôtres!

**The Priest:** *(voice trembling with indignation)*
Illness is a trial sent to purify hearts, not a matter of mops and buckets! You chase away prayer to put in lint and herbal tea!

**The Midwife:** *(stopping abruptly, pointing at him)*
Fever does not make theology, father! When the first feverish people arrive from the bottom of the canton spitting black blood, will you read them the book of Psalms? Will you tell them to pray while their lungs fill with water? I need three camp beds, clean linen, and a dry place to boil my instruments. Your presbytery has three straight chimneys and a damp-free floor. The ambulance will be here.

**The Priest:**
This is my house! Ecclesiastical authority...

**The Midwife:** *(filling a bowl with clear water which she places with a crash in front of him)*
It will be a haven of survival, father. Whether you like it or not. And if the Blessed Virgin passes through here again, she would rather find clean linen to wipe the foreheads of the dying than your books full of dust!

*(The Priest looks at her, his lips tight, unable to respond to her determination.)*
