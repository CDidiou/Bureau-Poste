*(The presbytery's large parish room. A long table of dark wood is crowded with holy pictures, candles and parish registers. The Priest is carefully folding his chasubles and putting away his missals. The door flies open. The Midwife enters with a large wicker basket under her arm, overflowing with linen sheets and dried herbs, followed by the Village Constable, carrying two large wooden tubs and rolls of lint.)*

**The Priest :** *(startled by the commotion, stepping in front of the table)*
What is the meaning of this racket, Mademoiselle? This isn't the market!

**The Midwife :**
Put those down on the benches, Constable.

**The Priest :**
I need those benches for catechism.

**The Midwife :**
Catechism can wait until the lungs of the district stop wheezing! Constable, you can go. Thank you for your help.

**The Village Constable :** *(wiping the sweat from his brow with his sleeve, hesitating)*
With all due respect, Mademoiselle... (He casts an uneasy glance at the Priest, lowers his head and hurries out.)

**The Priest :**
This is sacrilege, Mademoiselle! This room is reserved for the works of the parish and the comfort of souls! You cannot turn the presbytery into an infirmary without the bishopric's permission!

**The Midwife :** *(dumping her bundles of linen onto the table, pushing the candles aside)*
Your souls, Monsieur le Curé, will have plenty of time to burn in Purgatory if their bodies are choking on their own phlegm by the end of the week! Hot water and cresyl — that's what we need! Not prayers!

**The Priest :** *(his voice trembling with indignation)*
Disease is a trial sent to purify the heart, not a matter of mops and wash tubs! You are driving out prayer and replacing it with lint and herbal tea!

**The Midwife :** *(stopping dead and pointing a finger at him)*
Fever doesn't do theology, Father! When the first cases come in from the lower district coughing black blood, will you read them the Book of Psalms? Will you tell them to pray while their lungs fill with fluid? I need three camp beds, clean linen, and somewhere dry to boil my instruments. Your presbytery has three chimneys that draw properly and a floor without a trace of damp. The ambulance will come here.

**The Priest :**
It's my home! The ecclesiastical authority—

**The Midwife :** *(filling a bowl with clean water and setting it down in front of him with a bang)*
It will be a place of survival, Father. Whether you like it or not. And if the Blessed Virgin happens to pass this way, she'd rather find clean linen here to wipe the brows of the dying than your dust-covered books!

*(The Priest looks at her, lips pressed tightly together, unable to answer in the face of her determination.)*
