*(Mathers's stable. A suffocating, sticky heat reigns in the room lit only by the pale light of a kerosene lantern. Outside, the storm is raging and the rain is lashing furiously at the windows. Mathers's wife is lying on the straw, pale, clinging with all her strength to the sides of a cart. Mathers is motionless against the wall, his fork clutched against him, his gaze haggard. Country Guard, terrified, holds his breath near the door leaf. The Lame Man sits on a tub, overcome by coughing spasms which he tries to stifle in his jacket. The Midwife, her arms soaked up to the elbows, slowly moves away from the mother's body. In her hands, wrapped in a white linen cloth, lies a small inert form.

**The Lame Man :** *(between coughs, foaming with rage)*
Miserable fool! Were you doing all this mystery for a timpani and an egg cup? Looting items, on top of that! So you have no shame?

**Mathers :** *(exhausted)*
Did you think I was a thief or receiver of the Factory? I didn't bring much back from Verdun. But this silver tumbler and this knife wrapped in linen, I got them from the ruins of a castle in the North. I kept them hidden under the straw for the day my kid would have water poured over his head by the priest. So that he has at least one rich thing to his name.

**The Midwife :** *(voice broken, eyes fixed, whispering into space)*
It's... It's a girl. She didn't take her breath... She didn't even open her eyes.

**Mathers :** *(hoarse voice, taking two steps forward, fork trembling)*
What are you saying? A girl? And... and she's not screaming? Why isn't she screaming, Nurse?

**The Midwife :** *(bursting into tears, exhausted, her body bending with fatigue and despair)*
Because she left before coming, Mathers! Because the world is too rotten for an angel to breathe in it! I couldn't do anything... Nothing!

*(Mathers drops his fork into the straw with a dull thud.)*

**The Midwife :** *(in one breath, exhausted)*
In any case, it was a girl, your second Mathers. She only had a path of shadows before her.

*(At the same moment, the Lame Man is seized by a terrifying coughing fit which bends him in two. He puts a rag to his mouth and, when he removes it, a stain of black blood spreads in the hollow of the cloth. The Garde Champêtre retreats suddenly, uttering a cry of terror.)*

**The Village Constable :**
Blood! My God, the Lame is spitting blood! It's the plague, it's the flu! We're all screwed!

*(A heavy and terrifying silence falls on the stable. It is then that the Priest, until then prostrated in a corner of the stable, stands up. An almost ecstatic fervor shines in his eyes. He advances into the middle of the circle, his hand raised towards the frame, his voice rising, powerful and vibrant, covering the noise of the thunder.)*

**The priest :**
Look at you! Look what you have become! Frightened animals who count their bags of grain, officials who threaten their brothers with guns, men who bargain for the land when death knocks! You look for the curse in the air, but it is in your narrow hearts and in your pride!

*(The Midwife looks up at the Priest. The priest's words hit her hard, piercing the thick layer of her fatigue and sorrow.)*

**The priest :**
*(his voice is infinitely sweet, charged with celestial light)*
Listen to me! This world is only a valley of tears because we built it with our resentments and our barriers! But God did not create man to die alone in mud and distrust! There is a kingdom... A harmony higher than our miseries! In Paradise, my brothers, there is no land registry, no borders, no disease. Up there, men do not argue over the shade of a meadow or the weight of a harvest. Up there, every soul recognizes the beauty of its brother, hearts open without fear, and tears are wiped away by the pure clarity of universal love!

*(The Priest gently places his hand on the head of the inert child in the arms of the Midwife, his eyes wet with tears.)*

**The priest :**
This little girl will never know fear, nor greed, nor hatred. She is already sleeping in this garden of harmony where we should all live if we knew how to love each other! Why wait for heaven to be brothers? Why not join hands, here, now, to make this earth the reflection of its light? Join forces! Forgive yourself! Be good to one another, for there is no other treasure under the vault of heaven than love and fraternity!*(The Priest continues to pray in a low voice, his motionless silhouette illuminated by the lantern. Mathers collapses on his knees in the straw alongside his wife, the Country Guard lowers his head, and the Lame Wipes the sweat from his forehead, his gaze lost. The Midwife, motionless, hugs the infant to her chest. The Priest's sermon resonates in his mind like a burning revelation. His eyes fill with tears, no longer of despair, but of a painful certainty: this perfect world, this harmony that the Curé has just depicted, the men here are incapable of living it.)*
