**(Mathurin’s cowshed. A suffocating, clammy heat hangs in the air, lit only by the sickly glow of a paraffin lantern. Outside, the storm rages and rain lashes furiously against the windows. Mathurin’s wife lies on the straw, deathly pale, gripping the sides of a cart with all her strength. Mathurin stands motionless against the wall, pitchfork clutched to his chest, his eyes vacant. The Village Constable, terrified, holds his breath beside the half-open door.)**

**(The Cripple sits on an upturned bucket, seized by spasms of coughing which he tries to smother in his jacket. The Midwife, her arms wet to the elbows, slowly steps away from the mother’s body. In her hands, wrapped in a white linen cloth, lies a tiny, motionless form.)**

**The Cripple :** *(between coughs, foaming with rage)*
You miserable fool! All this secrecy over a silver cup and an egg cup? Looted goods, at that! Have you no shame?

**Mathurin :** *(at the end of his strength)*
You thought I was a thief, or a receiver of stolen goods from the parish? I didn’t bring much back from Verdun. But this silver cup and the knife wrapped in linen — I pulled them from the ruins of a château in the North. I kept them hidden under the straw for the day my boy would have water poured over his head by the Priest. So he’d have at least one fine thing to his name.

**The Midwife :** *(her voice broken, staring into nothing, whispering)*
It’s… It’s a girl. She didn’t draw breath… She didn’t even open her eyes.

**Mathurin :** *(hoarsely, taking two unsteady steps forward, his pitchfork trembling)*
What did you say? A girl? And… and she isn’t crying? Why isn’t she crying, Midwife?

**The Midwife :** *(bursting into tears, exhausted, her body buckling beneath fatigue and despair)*
Because she was gone before she ever came, Mathurin! Because the world is too rotten for an angel to breathe in! I couldn’t do anything… Nothing!

*(Mathurin lets his pitchfork fall into the straw with a dull thud.)*

**The Midwife :** *(in a whisper, utterly spent)*
It was a girl, after all. Your second daughter, Mathurin. There was nothing ahead of her but a road into darkness.

*(At that very moment, the Cripple is seized by a terrifying coughing fit that doubles him over. He presses a rag to his mouth and, when he pulls it away, a smear of black blood spreads across the linen. The Village Constable recoils with a cry of horror.)*

**The Village Constable :**
Blood! My God, the Cripple’s coughing up blood! It’s the plague, it’s influenza! We’re all done for!

*(A heavy, terrifying silence falls over the cowshed. Then the Priest, who until now has been kneeling prostrate in a corner, rises. A near-ecstatic fervour shines in his eyes. He steps into the centre of the circle, one hand raised towards the rafters, his voice powerful and vibrant, drowning out the thunder.)*

**The Priest :**
Look at yourselves! Look at what you’ve become! Frightened beasts counting their sacks of grain, officials threatening their brothers with rifles, men haggling over land while death is knocking at the window! You search for the curse in the air, but it lies in your narrow hearts and your pride!

*(The Midwife looks up at the Priest. His words strike her full force, piercing the thick layer of fatigue and grief that has settled over her.)*

**The Priest :** *(his voice softening into infinite tenderness, filled with a celestial light)*
Listen to me! This world is nothing but a vale of tears because we built it with our resentments and our barriers! But God did not make man to die alone in the mud, mistrusting his neighbour! There is a kingdom… A higher harmony beyond our misery! In Heaven, my brothers, there is no land registry, no border, no sickness. Up there, men do not quarrel over the shadow of a meadow or the weight of a harvest.

Up there, every soul recognises the beauty in his brother, hearts open without fear, and tears are wiped away by the pure radiance of universal love!

*(The Priest gently lays his hand upon the head of the lifeless child in the Midwife’s arms, his eyes wet with tears.)*

**The Priest :**
This little girl will never know fear, nor greed, nor hatred. She already sleeps in that garden of harmony where we should all live, if only we knew how to love one another! Why wait for Heaven to become brothers? Why not join hands, here and now, and make this earth a reflection of its light? Join your strength! Forgive one another! Be good to one another, for there is no treasure beneath the vault of Heaven but love and brotherhood!

*(The Priest continues praying under his breath, his motionless figure illuminated by the lantern. Mathurin collapses to his knees in the straw beside his wife. The Village Constable lowers his head, and the Cripple wipes the sweat from his brow, his gaze lost somewhere beyond the room. The Midwife stands motionless, clutching the newborn to her breast. The Priest’s sermon echoes through her mind like a searing revelation.)*

*(Her eyes fill with tears, no longer tears of despair, but of a painful certainty: this perfect world, this harmony the Priest has just described — the people here are incapable of living it.)*
