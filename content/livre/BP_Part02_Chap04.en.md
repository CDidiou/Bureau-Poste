*(The presbytery, at nightfall. A low-ceilinged room, its walls lined with shelves of leather-bound registers, their dark bindings swallowing almost all the lantern light — except now and then, when a gilt spine catches a brief glint, like an eye blinking in the shadows. On the great oak table, the lanterns cast a warm, living circle of light, an amber island in the middle of the room, while the faint smoke from their wicks curls upwards in shapes that seem to dance rather than rise.)*

*(The letter unearthed from the diocesan archives, the little account book, the box of seals and the old cadastral map are spread across this circle of light like a constellation laid out upon the table. Philip bends over them, compass in hand, his movements slow and precise, like an enchanter drawing a circle. Zephyrine stands at his side, as always, her shawl catching the golden reflections of the flames. The Priest remains apart beside his shelves, seemingly reassured to be on his own ground.)*

*(Mathurin and the Village Constable wait standing at the edge of the halo of light — one with his arms folded, the other shifting impatiently.)*

**Philip :** *(drawing a line with his pencil, his voice vibrant, as though carried by the very light of the lanterns)*
Look, my friends! The number on this seal — seventeen sixty-one — is the very same number written in the margin of the letter Father found! And when you cross-reference that number with the parish land records, it points to one very precise entry… *(he points to a spot on the map, where the nearest flame seems to bend for a moment to illuminate his finger)* … here.

The old boundary marker. The plum tree.

*(A silence in which even the smoke from the wicks seems to suspend its dance. Then Mathurin takes a heavy step towards the table, leaving the warm circle of light before re-entering it against the light, his shadow suddenly spreading enormous across the wall of registers.)*

**Father Mathurin :**
The plum tree. *(A pause.)* My plum tree.

**Philip :** *(without looking up, still absorbed, his voice retaining its enchanted gentleness)*
On your land, yes, Mathurin. By some twist of fate, the secret has slept beneath your—

**Father Mathurin :** *(his voice suddenly rising, cutting Philip off and shattering the beautiful melody of the moment)*
Fate had nothing to do with it! It’s my land! My tree! And whatever is sleeping beneath it belongs to me, and nobody else!

**The Brave Constable :** *(trying a joke to ease the tension)*
Come on, Mathurin, nobody’s saying otherwise—

**Father Mathurin :** *(turning on him threateningly)*
You, for one, shut your trap! I saw the two of you looking at each other earlier, whispering as though it had already been decided, as though my property were going to be divided between you like bread at Mass!

**Zephyrine :** *(stepping forward, her hand still resting on Philip’s arm, her voice calm, trying to draw some of the lanterns’ warmth around her)*
Mathurin. Nobody is taking anything from you. If this gold exists, it is your land that has kept it for a hundred years, faithfully, like a nursemaid guarding a child’s secret. It has earned our gratitude.

**Father Mathurin :** *(still angry)*
Gratitude! I don’t need gratitude. I need you to leave me what is mine! I’m sick of people making decisions for me, behind my back, around a table that isn’t even mine!

*(He slams his palm down on the map, making the lanterns flicker; the shadows of the registers twist across the wall for a moment as though seized by a shudder. The Priest instinctively closes a register that has been left open, as if to protect it from this sudden gust of anger.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(detaching herself from Philip for a moment to place a hand on Mathurin’s shoulder, gently but firmly, while the light seems to draw tighter around the two of them as though enclosing them)*
Look at me. Nobody here has decided anything without you. We came for you first, before we even came to the presbytery. You have as much claim to this treasure as the plum tree itself.

**Father Mathurin :** *(unmoved, jaw clenched)*
Words. Always words, with you people. I want a promise. A real one.

*(Zephyrine exchanges a glance with Philip — a look that seems, for an instant, to catch all the amber light in the room — then turns back to Mathurin, closer now, her voice lower, almost confidential.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(a second attempt)*
Then here is my promise: nothing will come out from beneath that plum tree without you, without your spade, without your consent for every clod of earth we turn. You will open this treasure with your own hands, Mathurin, beneath the same sky that watched over your ancestors. No one will steal it from you, not even in thought.

*(Mathurin remains silent for a moment, his breathing still short, but something imperceptibly loosens in his shoulders — as though the warmth of the lanterns has finally reached him too. He grumbles, without quite giving in.)*

**Father Mathurin :**
We’ll see. We’ll see whether words hold once the earth has been turned.

*(Then the Village Constable, who has remained in the background throughout the quarrel, suddenly straightens as though struck by an idea that has surprised even him — so abruptly that he nearly knocks over one of the lanterns.)*

**The Brave Constable :** *(puffing out his chest)*
Well, I know what we ought to do! Until we dig, this notebook and these seals are sensitive materials! I’m requisitioning them, in the name of public order!

**Philip :** *(finally looking up, surprised, the spell momentarily broken)*
Requisitioning them? But nobody asked you to—

**The Brave Constable :** *(already gathering the notebook, the box of seals and the letter into the folds of his jacket, with an authority no one has ever seen in him, stepping out of the amber circle of light as one might step out of a dream)*
Exactly! That’s why someone has to think of it! I’ll take them to the post office, under lock and key, in the drawer of the official register. There, at least, nobody will be able to say we favoured one side or the other!

**Father Mathurin :** *(stiffening again)*
The post office? And why not my house, directly, since it’s my plum tree we’re talking about?

**The Brave Constable :** *(carried away, almost solemn)*
Precisely because it’s your plum tree, Mathurin! Order exists to protect people like you from people like… *(he hesitates, searching for the words)* … well, from everyone, really!

*(A tense silence. The smoke from the lanterns, agitated for a moment, gradually resumes its peaceful dance. Zephyrine, once again at Philip’s side, watches the scene with sharp attention — a worried crease crosses her brow, quickly erased like a cloud-shadow passing over still water.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(a third attempt, addressing Mathurin in a gentle but unwavering voice)*
Let him do it, Mathurin. It’s only for one night, under lock and key, until we are all ready to open the earth together, in broad daylight, before witnesses, beneath the kindly gaze of the whole village. No one will touch your plum tree without you. I swear it, on what I hold most dear.

*(As she says this, she presses Philip’s arm a little more tightly against her, and the lantern light, reassured, seems to settle more gently upon them. Mathurin looks from Zephyrine to the Constable, who is finishing pocketing the last objects, and then to the map spread across the table. At last, reluctantly, he nods.)*

**Father Mathurin :** *(grumbling)*
One night. Not a night more. And I want to see that drawer locked with my own eyes.

**The Brave Constable :** *(already at the door, proud as a peacock)*
You shall, Mathurin! I swear it on my drum!

*(He almost runs out into the night, arms full, carrying with him for a moment some of the amber light which seems to follow him to the threshold before closing again over the darkness outside. Mathurin follows him with stiff steps, without looking back. The Priest, who has remained beside his shelves, gently closes the letter inside his register, still troubled by what he has set in motion without meaning to.)*

**The Priest :** *(under his breath, almost to himself)*
I went looking for a trace in the dust. I never thought it would make so many hands tremble.

**Zephyrine :** *(turning towards him, still holding Philip’s arm, with a soothing smile, while the last lantern seems to flare brighter around them)*
Hands may tremble, Father, but they have not been raised against one another. That is already much, for one evening.

*(Philip places his hand over Zephyrine’s hand, resting on his arm, and holds it there for a moment before they too leave the trembling lantern light of the presbytery — leaving the room to sink gently back into its silence of registers and dark leather.)*
