*(The presbytery, at nightfall. A low room, with walls covered with shelves of bound registers, the dark leather of which absorbs almost all the light from the lanterns - except at moments, when a golden binding lights up with a brief flash, like an eye blinking in the shadows. On the large oak table, the lanterns draw a warm and living circle, an island of amber light in the middle of the room, and the light smoke from the wicks rises in volutes which seem to dance more than they rise. The letter exhumed from the diocesan archives, the small account book, the box of stamps and the old cadastral map are spread out on this circle of light, like a constellation placed on the table. Joseph is leaning over it, a compass in his hand, his precise and slow movements like those of an enchanter tracing a circle, as always. capturing the golden reflections of the flames The Curé stands back near his shelves, as if reassured to be at home. Mathurin and the Garde Champêtre wait, standing at the edge of this halo of light - one with his arms crossed, the other stamping his feet.)*

**Joseph:** *(tracing a line with a pencil, his voice vibrating, as if carried by the very light of the lanterns)* Look, my friends! The number on this stamp — seventeen sixty-one — is the same number that appears in the margin of the letter found by the Cure! And this figure, once crossed with the factory's land register, refers to a very specific paragraph... *(he points to a place on the map, where the nearest flame seems to bend for a moment to illuminate his finger)* ... here. The old boundary. The plum tree.

*(A silence, where even the smoke from the wicks seems to suspend its dance. Then Mathurin advances with heavy steps towards the table, abruptly leaving the circle of warm light to re-enter it against the light, his shadow suddenly spreading out, immense, on the wall of registers.)*

**Mathurin:** The plum tree. *(Pause.)* My plum tree.

**Joseph:** *(without looking up, still absorbed, his voice retaining its enchanted softness)* On your plot, yes, Mathurin. As luck would have it, the secret has been lying dormant in your home ever since...

**Mathurin:** *(the voice rising suddenly, cutting Joseph off short, abruptly breaking the beautiful melody of the moment)* Chance didn't want anything at all! This is my land! It's my tree! And everything that sleeps underneath belongs to me, and no one else!

**The Garde Champêtre:** *(attempting a joke to lighten the air)* Come on, Mathurin, no one says the opposite...

**Mathurin:** *(turning towards him, threatening)* You, the first, shut up! I saw you looking at each other earlier, whispering as if it was already decided, as if my property was going to be divided among you like bread at mass!

**Zéphyrine:** *(advancing, hand still on Joseph's arm, voice calm, trying to bring back around her a little of the warmth of the lanterns)* Mathurin. Nobody takes anything from you. This gold, if it exists, it is your land which has guarded it for a century, faithfully, like a nurse keeps a child's secret. She deserved to be grateful to you.

**Mathurin:** *(without calming down)* Grateful! I don't need recognition, I need to be given what is due to me! I'm tired of people deciding for me, behind my back, around a table that isn't even mine!

*(He taps the flat of his hand on the card, making the lanterns flicker; the shadows of the registers on the wall twist for a moment as if gripped by a shiver. The Priest instinctively closes a register that remains open, as if to protect it from this sudden gust of wind.)*

**Zéphyrine:** *(detaching herself from Joseph's arm for a moment to place her hand on Mathurin's shoulder, gently but firmly, while the light seems to tighten around them both as if to envelop them)* Look at me. No one here has decided anything without you. We came to get you first, even before coming to the presbytery. You are as much a part of this treasure as the plum tree itself.

**Mathurin:** *(without letting himself bend, jaw clenched)* Words. Always words, at home. I want a promise. A real one.

*(Zéphyrine exchanges a look with Joseph – a look which, for a moment, seems to capture all the amber light in the room – then returns to Mathurin, this time closer, his voice lower, almost confidential.)*

**Zéphyrine:** *(second attempt)* So here is my promise: nothing will come out from under this plum tree without you, without your shovel, without your agreement on each clod of earth that we lift. You will open this treasure with your own hands, Mathurin, under the same sky that watched over your ancestors. No one will steal it from you, not even in thought.*(Mathurin remains silent for a moment, his breathing still short, but something, imperceptibly, loosens in his shoulders - as if the heat of the lanterns, finally, reached him a little, too. He grumbles, without completely giving in.)*

**Mathurin:** We'll see. We will see if the words hold, once the earth is turned over.

*(It is then that the Garde Champêtre, who had remained behind during the scuffle, suddenly stands up, as if struck by an idea that is beyond his control - a gesture so abrupt that he almost knocks over one of the lanterns.)*

**The Country Guard:** *(puffing his chest)* Well, I know what to do! As long as we don't dig, this notebook and these stamps are sensitive items! I am requisitioning them, in the name of public order!

**Joseph:** *(finally looking up, surprised, the spell broken for a moment)* Requisition them? But after all, no one asked you anything...

**The Country Guard:** *(already gathering the notebook, the box of stamps and the letter in the flaps of his jacket, with an authority that we don't know about him, coming out of the circle of amber light like coming out of a dream)* Exactly! That’s why someone needs to think about it! I take them to the post office, under lock and key, in the official register drawer. There, at least, no one will be able to say that we favored one or the other!

**Mathurin:** *(stiffening again)* At the post office? And why not at my house, directly, since it’s my plum tree we’re talking about!

**The Garde Champêtre:** *(on his way, almost solemn)* Precisely because it's your plum tree, Mathurin! Order serves to protect people like you from people like… *(he hesitates, searching for words)* … well, from everyone, that is!

*(A tense silence, during which the smoke from the lanterns, agitated for a moment, gradually regains its tranquil dance. Zéphyrine, once again on Joseph's arm, observes the scene with keen attention - a worried crease crosses her forehead, quickly erased, like the shadow of a cloud on calm water.)*

**Zéphyrine:** *(third attempt, speaking to Mathurin in a soft but not trembling voice)* Let him do it, Mathurin. It's only one night, under lock and key, while we wait until we are all ready to open the earth together, in broad daylight, in front of witnesses, under the benevolent gaze of the entire village. No one will touch your plum tree without you. I swear to you, on what is dearest to me.

*(As she says this, she hugs Joseph's arm a little tighter against her, and the light of the lanterns, as if reassured, seems to fall more gently on them both. Mathurin looks in turn at Zéphyrine, the Guard who is finishing pocketing the last objects, then the map spread out on the table. He ends up nodding his head, reluctantly.)*

**Mathurin:** *(grumbling)* One night. Not one more. And I want to see this drawer lock with my own eyes.

**The Country Guard:** *(already at the door, proud as a rooster)* You will see him, Mathurin! I swear to you on my drum!

*(He goes out almost running into the night, his arms full, carrying with him, for a moment, a little of the amber light which seems to follow him to the threshold before closing in on the darkness outside. Mathurin follows him with a stiff step, without a backward glance. The Curé, remaining near his shelves, gently closes the letter in his register, still troubled by what he has just provoked without having wanted to.)*

**The Priest:** *(in a low voice, as if to himself)* I looked for a trace in the dust. I didn't think it would make so many hands shake.

**Zephyrine:** *(turning towards him, still on Joseph's arm, with a soothing smile, while the last lantern seems to revive its radiance around them)* The hands tremble, My Father, but they are not raised against each other. That's already a lot for just one evening.

*(Joseph places his hand on Zéphyrine's, resting on his arm, and holds her against him for a moment before they, in turn, leave the trembling light of the presbytery lanterns - leaving behind them the room falls, gently, into its silence of registers and dark leather.)*
