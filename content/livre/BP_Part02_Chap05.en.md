*(A patch of woodland on the edge of the district. The night is clear, pricked with stars that glitter like diamonds beneath a radiant moon. A gentle breeze makes the leaves of the old oaks sing, and the light music seems to accompany every step of the little procession. The Village Constable walks at the head, carrying a copper lantern whose golden glow lights the way.)*

*(Behind him comes Philip — brisk and alert — holding Zephyrine by the hand, followed by the Priest and Mathurin, each carrying, with a certain restrained pride, the surveying tools.)*

**The Brave Constable :** *(stopping before the old plum tree, whose shadow seems almost to breathe beneath the moon)*
Here we are, my good friends! Right here, beneath the roots. I’ve known every inch of this hollow since I was seven — if the captain’s notebook tells the truth, this is where the clasp on memory will finally spring open.

**The Priest :** *(coming closer, his voice still carrying something of the solemnity of the presbytery)*
Look how the light seems to linger on this tree more than on the others. Providence is guiding our steps, my children.

**Zephyrine :** *(gently squeezing Philip’s arm, her eyes raised towards the silvered branches)*
Philip, the line you drew on the map… Is this where it meets the plum tree’s gaze?

**Philip :** *(stepping forward, laying a hand on the gnarled bark)*
Exactly beneath that root, Zephyrine. My mind never doubted it for a moment. “Where faith shelters the traveller, memory lies at rest” — that is what the notebook said. Brave Constable, bring your lantern down to the foot of the tree.

**The Brave Constable :** *(obeying, lowering the light)*
Look! A flat stone, barely covered in moss!

*(Mathurin steps forward in turn, kneels without a word, and clears the stone with his bare hands. It gives way almost effortlessly, revealing a small hollow lined with dry earth. At the bottom, a round metal object, polished despite the years, glints in the moonlight.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(a delighted breath)*
A medal!

*(But scarcely has she named it when Mathurin closes his rough hand around the object and rises abruptly, his face set.)*

**Father Mathurin :** *(his voice hoarse and sharp)*
It’s been sleeping on my land for a hundred years. My stone, my earth, my sweat keeping it tended year after year. So let’s be clear this time: whatever is in there — gold, silver, all of it — belongs to me. To me alone.

**Philip :** *(taken aback)*
Mathurin, we already agreed about this at the presbytery…

**Father Mathurin :** *(cutting him off, his voice rising)*
At the presbytery, I said, “We’ll see.” Well, we’ve seen. And what I see is five of you turning up on my land for a stone that I was the one to lift!

**The Brave Constable :** *(stepping forward, the lantern trembling slightly in his hand, but his voice firm)*
And he’s not wrong, Mathurin! Let me tell you something plain: between a farmer and a constable who’ve worked themselves to the bone all their lives, and men of letters and the cloth who know how to read the fine print… I know whose side I’m on!

**Philip :** *(astonished)*
You too, now?

**The Brave Constable :** *(arms folded, almost proud of his audacity)*
Us? We’ve never had anything except orders to obey and fines to hand out. If there really is gold beneath our feet at last, then it ought to go to those who’ve sweated over this soil — not to those who merely know how to talk prettily about it!

**The Priest :** *(stepping between them abruptly, hands raised to hold them back)*
Enough! We don’t even know that it is gold — look at what you’re holding, Mathurin! It isn’t a coin, it’s a consecrated medal! It belongs to the Church, and to the Church alone! To pass it from hand to hand on bare earth would be sacrilege!

**Father Mathurin :** *(clenching his fist tighter)*
Sacrilege or not, it was beneath my soil, Father. Not beneath your altar.

**The Priest :** *(his voice rising, unusually hard)*
It belongs to God before it belongs to anyone, Mathurin! Give it to me, I beg you, before your hands turn it into a cause of discord rather than a sign of grace!

*(The breeze, which had been singing softly through the oaks, seems to stiffen too; the leaves stir with a new nervousness. The voices overlap — Mathurin growling, the Constable joining in, the Priest raising his voice in turn — a tangle of accusations and old grievances rising, rising, with no one willing to yield.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(suddenly, her voice cracking like a whip, so unlike her usual gentleness that everyone freezes)*
ENOUGH!

*(A brutal silence falls over the clearing. Even the moon seems, for a moment, to hold its breath. Zephyrine stands straight, her expression hard, her eyes sweeping over all four men.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(her voice trembling with anger, but every word measured)*
You are quarrelling over a saint’s medal beneath a plum tree, like children fighting over a toy — you, Mathurin, who promised to open this treasure “for the memory of the brave”! You, my brave Constable, who swore on your drum to keep the peace, and now you’re stirring it up yourself! And you, Father, who preached this very morning that History brings us together rather than setting us against one another!

*(She steps forward and almost tears the medal from Mathurin’s fingers — startled, he lets her take it without resistance — and holds it out before her, at arm’s length, like evidence in a trial.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(more quietly, but no less sharply)*
This medal belongs to no one, or it belongs to all of us. It has waited a hundred years beneath this root without the Church, the land, or the law coming to claim it. So tonight, it will wait a little longer, until you remember who you truly are, each of you, before it reveals anything at all.

*(A long silence follows. Mathurin is the first to lower his eyes, his jaw still tight but his anger subsiding. The Village Constable scratches the back of his neck, embarrassed, his earlier confidence gone. The Priest slowly folds his hands, as though gathering himself after the outburst.)*

**Father Mathurin :** *(his voice softened, almost sheepish)*
I never said I wouldn’t share it, Zephyrine. I said I was afraid we’d forget me, once the stone was lifted.

**The Brave Constable :** *(nodding, sheepish now too)*
Same here. Perhaps the night has made me lose my head. And a little old resentment as well.

**The Priest :** *(quietly)*
And I was afraid for God, when what I ought to fear is what men are capable of.

**Philip :** *(stepping forward, laying a calming hand on Mathurin’s shoulder, then on the Constable’s)*
Then let it be said once and for all, here before this tree and beneath these stars: no one here walks alone towards this treasure. We go together, or we do not go at all.

**Zephyrine :** *(gradually recovering her customary gentleness, holding the medal up to the lantern so that they can all see it at once)*
There. That looks more like us.

*(She takes Philip’s arm again, and this time she also beckons the Priest, Mathurin and the Constable closer with her eyes, until they form a tight circle around the medal. A moment of calm, almost solemn.)*

*(But as the little group stands there, united, the moon, which had shone so brilliantly moments before, seems to have lost some of its strength — its light on their faces is suddenly paler, greyer, as though washed out by the storm that has just passed between them. Without their noticing, the stars, too, are fewer now, and less bright.)*
