*(An undergrowth on the edge of the canton. The night is clear, dotted with stars that sparkle like diamonds under a radiant moon. A gentle breeze makes the leaves of the old oaks sing, and this light music seems to accompany each step of the little procession. The Garde Champêtre walks at the head, holding a copper lantern whose golden light illuminates the path. Behind him Philip advances - with a quick and alert step - holding Zephyrine by the hand, followed by the Curé and Mathers, who each carry, not without a certain suppressed pride, the surveying tools.)*

**The Brave Guard :** *(stopping in front of the old plum tree, whose shadow almost seems to breathe under the moon)* Here we are, my brave friends! Right here, above the roots. I have known every mound of this valley since I was seven years old - if the captain's notebook is true, this is where the clasp of memory will open.

**The Priest :** *(approaching, his voice still imbued with the solemnity of the presbytery)* See how the light seems to linger on this tree more than on the others. Providence guides our steps, my children.

**Zephyrine :** *(tenderly hugging Philip's arm, looking up at the silvery branches)* Philip, the alignment that you traced on the map... Is this where the line meets the gaze of the plum tree?

**Philip :** *(stepping forward, placing his hand on the gnarled bark)* Exactly under that root, Zephyrine. My mind did not doubt for a moment. “Where faith protects the traveler, memory rests” — so spoke the notebook. Brave Guard, tilt your lantern towards the base of the tree.

**The Brave Guard :** *(obediently, approaching the light)* Look! A flat stone, barely covered with moss!

*(Mathers steps forward in turn, kneels without a word, and removes the stone with his bare hands. It gives way almost effortlessly, revealing a small cavity lined with dry earth. At the bottom, a round metal object, polished despite the years, shimmers in the light of the moon.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(a delighted breath)* A medal!

*(But no sooner has she named it than Mathers closes his calloused hand on the object and stands up straight, his face closed.)*

**Mathers :** *(hoarse, sharp voice)* It's been sleeping in my house for a century. It is my stone, my land, my sweat to maintain it year after year. So this time, let's be clear: what's in there, the gold, the silver, everything — it's mine. To me alone.

**Philip :** *(disconcerted)* Mathers, we have already agreed on this at the presbytery…

**Mathers :** *(cutting him off, his voice swelling)* At the presbytery, I said “we’ll see”. We saw. And above all I see that there are five of you who have invited yourselves to my land for a stone that I lifted myself!

**The Brave Guard :** *(advances in turn, the lantern trembling a little in his hand, but his voice firm)* And he is not wrong, Mathers! Here, I'll tell you straight: between a peasant and a guard who have always toiled, and people of letters and cassocks who know how to read the paragraphs... I know which side I'm on!

**Philip :** *(stunned)* You too, now?

**The Brave Guard :** *(arms crossed, almost proud of his audacity)* Me too! The rest of us never had anything except orders and fines to distribute. If the gold is finally there, under our feet, may it go to those who have sweated on this earth - not to those who only know how to talk about it nicely!

**The Priest :** *(interposing abruptly, hands raised in a barrage)* Enough! It's not even about gold right now — look what you're holding, Mathers! It's not a coin, it's a consecrated medal! It belongs to the domain of the Church, and to it alone! Letting it pass from hand to hand on bare earth would be sacrilege!

**Mathers :** *(squeezing her even tighter in his fist)* Sacrilege or not, she was under my earth, Father. Not under your altar.

**The Priest :** *(voice rising, unusually harsh)* It belongs to God before belonging to anyone, Mathers! Give it back to me, I beseech you, before your hands make it an object of discord rather than a sign of grace!

*(The breeze, which sang softly in the oaks, also seems to stiffen; the leaves move with a new nervousness. The voices are superimposed - Mathers who scolds, the Guard who adds, the Curé who raises his voice in turn - a disorder of reproaches and grievances which rises, rises, without any one giving way.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(suddenly, in a voice that cracks like a whip, so different from its usual gentleness that everyone freezes)* THAT IS ENOUGH!

*(A brutal silence falls over the clearing. Even the moon, for a moment, seems to hold its breath. Zephyrine stands straight, her gaze hard, scanning each of the four men with her gaze.)***Zephyrine :** *(voice vibrating with anger, but each word weighed)* You are fighting over a saint's medal under a plum tree, like children fighting over a toy - you, Mathers, who promised to open this treasure "for the memory of the brave"! You, my brave Guard, who swore on your drum to keep order, and here you are stirring it up! And you, my Father, who preached this very morning that History brings us together and not divides us!

*(She steps forward, almost snatches the medal from Mathers's fingers - who, seized, lets her do so without resisting - and holds it in front of her, at arm's length, as evidence against her.)*

**Zephyrine :** *(lower, but just as sharp)* This medal belongs to no one, or it belongs to all of us. She waited a hundred years under this root without neither the Church, nor the earth, nor the law coming to claim her. So tonight, she'll wait a moment longer for you to remember who you really are, each of you, before she reveals anything.

*(A long silence follows. Mathers looks down first, his jaw still clenched but the anger has subsided. The Garde Champêtre scratches the back of his neck, embarrassed, his confidence from earlier gone. The Curé, for his part, slowly folds his hands, as if to reflect after the scandal.)*

**Mathers :** *(in a softened, almost sheepish voice)* I didn't say that I refused to share, Zephyrine. I said I was afraid it would be forgotten once the stone was lifted.

**The Brave Guard :** *(nodding his head, sheepish in turn)* Me the same. It was the night that made me lose my mind, no doubt. And a little old grudge, too.

**The Priest :** *(in a low voice)* And I feared for God what I should fear for men.

**Philip :** *(stepping forward, placing a soothing hand on Mathers's shoulder, then on that of the Guard)* So let it be said once and for all, in front of this tree and under these stars: no one here walks alone towards this treasure. We go together, or we don't.

**Zephyrine :** *(recovering, little by little, her customary gentleness, holding out the medal to the light of the lantern so that everyone can see it at once)* There you go. This is more like us.

*(She takes Philip's arm again, and this time, it is she who, with her eyes, also invites the Curé, Mathers and the Guard to come closer, until they form a tight circle around the medal. A moment of calm, almost solemn. But while the small group stands like this, united, the moon, which was shining so radiantly earlier, seems to have lost a little of its strength - its light, on the faces, is suddenly more pale, grayer, as if washed out by the storm that has just passed between them. Without them noticing it, the stars, too, are a little less numerous, a little less vivid.)*
