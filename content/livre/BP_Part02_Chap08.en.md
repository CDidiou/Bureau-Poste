*(Mathers's large kitchen, late at night. The fire in the hearth has calmed down into glowing embers, but the entire room seems to be bathed in a light that no longer quite belongs to a single candle - a diffuse, almost suspended light, as if the party itself had begun to glow from within. The table is cluttered with glasses and empty bottles. The priest, his cassock unbuttoned, has rolled up his sleeves. The Garde Champêtre refills an inkwell with the concentration of a diligent schoolboy. Philip holds the quill pen, Zephyrine standing behind him, one hand placed on his shoulder.)*

**Mathers :** *(refilling the Guard's pipe with a broad smile, his voice having lost all trace of the anger of the previous days)* Add a line on the plum tree, Philip! Say that under the roots, the captain had engraved the canton's motto with the tip of his saber - and that it belongs to me, body and soul, without the commune having to stick its nose in it!

**The Brave Guard :** *(banging his fist on the table in a burst of laughter, the inkwell almost tipping over)* And also that the constabulary searched for the fold for three days without ever finding the way, because I had moved the marker of La Combe by fifty toises!

**The Priest :** *(smiling, dipping a biscuit in his wine, his voice light as it has not been since the barn)* Do not overload the parchment with secular artifices, gentlemen! Let us say rather that Providence veiled the eyes of the prefecture's bloodhounds, to preserve the innocence of our valleys.

**Zephyrine :** *(leaning over Philip's shoulder, her voice enveloping the room with a softness that seems to make the candlelight flicker a little more)* Let them say it, Philip. Add all the romance of your cards, all the poetry of our nights, all the bravery of your ancestors. May those who read this story a hundred years from now have their hearts filled with wonder.

**Philip :** *(reading aloud again, his voice deep and full)* “*And this is how Philip and Zephyrine, guided by the faith of the priest, the loyalty of the guard and the greatness of the peasant, after having preserved the secret of the patriots of 1837, were united by the sacred bonds of marriage...*”

**The Brave Guard :** *(raising his glass high)* To the legend of Philip and Zephyrine!

**All :** To the legend!

*(Philip puts down the pen, gets up with a light jump - too light, almost that of a man younger than himself - and goes to rummage at the bottom of the old oak chest. He pulls out a pot-bellied bottle, powdered with dust, with a yellowed label.)*

**The Brave Guard :** By all the saints of the calendar! Muscat! Where the hell did you find such a marvel?

**Philip :** *(a wink to Mathers)* With Mathers, we have our secrets.

*(He pours. The golden liquid flows into the glasses with an almost unreal slowness, capturing all the light in the room, as if each drop held back a little sunshine from a summer that neither of them experienced together.)*

**The Priest :** *(eyes shining)* Ah, this is the language of carelessness. This evening, the sky descends into our glasses.

*(They toast. The sound of the crystal lingers longer than it should, like a held note. Mathers turns towards the cradle, where the child — whose face, this night, finally seems to have settled for good — lets out a soft sigh.)*

**Mathers :** *(dreamy voice)* To think that there was neither gold coins nor chest of louis to dig up... And I have never felt so rich in all my life.

**Zephyrine :** *(a radiant smile, but whose eyes, for a moment, seem to seek something beyond the circle of light)* The gold goes out, Mathers, and the earth is covered with snow. But this happiness never leaves us. We found the treasure on the first evening: it was to love each other, to forgive each other, and to be, despite everything, happy together.

*(Philip brings Zephyrine's hand to his lips. Around them, the kitchen has ceased, little by little, to be entirely a kitchen: the shelves fade into soft darkness, the stone walls dilute into a glow without precise outline, as if the entire decor had decided to retain only the essentials — the table, the glasses, the beloved faces.)*

**Philip :** *(a glass of muscat in his hand, a knowing smile)* You can see that we both ended up getting there. We have carved out the contours of our own history.

**Zephyrine :** *(laughing, a light, almost childish laugh)* Our own legend... Oh, my love.*(They toast again. The ringing continues, continues, almost musical, until it becomes a continuous note, like a thread stretched in the golden silence of the room. It is then that Zephyrine, without knowing why, turns her head towards the back of the kitchen. A door stands there, ajar - a door that, at this moment, she does not remember having ever seen in this place. The light, around her, no longer dances; it stands still, as if held in the opening, a figure stands, also motionless. Zephyrine recognizes it without any possible doubt. Philip, at this precise moment, puts down his glass. He stands up - with a slow, even movement, as if pulled by a thread. He does not say anything. He does not look at Zephyrine or anyone else. closes behind him without a sound. Zephyrine remains standing, the glass still in her hand. She does not move. She does not call to him. Her eyes fill slowly, and a tear, then another, falls silently down her face, without any sob coming to accompany them. — as if the door, the silhouette, and the sudden emptiness next to Zephyrine had never existed.)*
