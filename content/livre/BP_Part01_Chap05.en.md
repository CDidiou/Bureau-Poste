*(Dark night. The village is sealed off, doors locked and shutters closed for fear of bad air and contagion. The square is deserted. Only the regular lapping of the fountain breaks the silence. The Lame is leaning against the stone basin, his stick placed between his legs. A sound of light footsteps on the pavement makes him stand up. The Midwife emerges from the shadow of an alley, a dark shawl tight over her shoulders.)*

**The Midwife :** *(stopping, short of breath)*
You're crazy, Major... The guard is still making rounds despite the hour, and people are watching behind the shutters. If they see movement, they'll cry wolf.

**The Lame Man :** *(in a low, almost warm voice)*
The guard snoozes under his courtyard, the drum between his thighs. And the residents are too afraid of the fever to go outside.
*(Pause. He contemplates her.)*
I had to see you before everything shut down for good.

**The Midwife :** *(approaching the fountain)*
Tomorrow, the town hall barriers will be up. They are blocking the bottom passage. If La Mélie starts work on Sunday, I don't even know how I will cross the stream.

**The Lame Man :**
You will cross. If necessary, I will carry you on my shoulders, wooden leg first in the mud.

**The Midwife :** *(a little restrained laugh, immediately stifled under her hand)*
Shut up, let's see... Everyone already says that you dig under the plum trees at night. Don't give them anything to talk about anymore, I beg you.

**The Lame Man :**
Let them talk! All they have to do is wait for the sweat to take over. But you...
*(He takes a step towards her. The end of his crutch slips an inch on the damp stone. She holds out her hands to hold him by the front of his jacket. Their faces are a few inches apart. In the dark, the silence becomes heavier. The Lame Gently places his lips on hers — a brief, feverish, restrained kiss. At the same moment, a slight metallic shock sounds from the other side of the basin. A tin bucket comes to hit the stone edge A kid - the carpenter's little one, as tall as three apples, holding his water pot with both hands - remains petrified in the shadow of the washhouse, his eyes wide open.)*

**The Midwife :** *(sitting up suddenly, voice muffled)*
Jesus, Mary... Philip!

**The Lame Man :** *(without moving, staring at the kid without aggression, his tone sharp but low)*
Hey, kid. What are you doing outside at this hour? Does your mother know you're hanging around?

**The carpenter's little one :** *(stuttering, bucket shaking)*
It's... it's for the grown-up's herbal tea. She coughs red... My father told me to run to the well before curfew...

**The Midwife :** *(walking quickly towards the child, kneeling at his level)*
Give me that.
*(She gently takes the bucket from his hands)*
You need the clearest pond water.
*(She fills the pot in two precise gestures, gives it back to him, then looks straight into his gaze)*
You will go through the Ruelle des Deux-Frères. Without stopping. And if you tell anyone that you saw me here with the Major, I'll find something to anger your parents for good. Is that understood correctly?
*(The kid nods, terror in his eyes, grabs the pot with his two sweaty hands and runs away. The hasty clicking of his hooves quickly fades away in the dark alley.)*

**The Lame Man :** *(looking at the empty alley, a slight smile at the corner of his lips)*
Look at him! You scared him so much, my dear Zéphie, that he will have spilled half of it before crossing the river!

**The Midwife :** *(turning towards him, putting back her bun with a nervous gesture, her heart beating)*
It's not funny, major. Tomorrow, everyone in the lower canton will know.

**The Lame Man :** *(his tone softening)*
Let them speak. Tomorrow, they will have other fish to fry with their registers and their stretchers. Come home. It's too cold for you.
*(A silence settles. Their eyes meet in the dark. He makes a gesture, she approaches. They kiss — longer this time, a deep and restrained kiss that seals their alliance.)*
