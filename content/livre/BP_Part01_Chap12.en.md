*(The temporary post office. The place is crammed with stamps, municipal registers and official telegrams. The Major sits behind the great wooden counter. On the makeshift desk stands the ever-present bronze bust of Balthasar d’André, known as the Grocer. The inscription reads: Honorary Citizen, Illustrious Son of the Village. President of the Constituent Assembly. With an air of sovereign authority, the Major sorts through passes and requisition forms.)*

*(The Village Constable stands before him, kepi in hand. The Midwife enters, shawl around her shoulders, eyes bright.)*

**The Midwife :** *(approaching the desk, lowering her voice)*
Major… I need a pass for Mathurin’s farm. I have to make a check-up before the birth. I’m worried.

**The Cripple :** *(without looking up from his stamps)*
I know. But I can’t sign one. The area is under quarantine order.

**The Midwife :** *(with a slightly forced smile, trying to catch his eye)*
Women carry life, and their pains have little patience for administrative dithering. I need that pass. Urgently.

**The Cripple :** *(turning sharply to the Village Constable)*
And you, Constable? Did you do as I told you?

**The Village Constable :** *(coming closer, speaking under his breath, a mischievous glint in his eye)*
I’ve just come back, Major. He’d barred the door with great lengths of timber, but I’ve got an eye for these things, you know!

**The Cripple :** *(coming to a halt, his gaze suddenly razor-sharp)*
And?

**The Village Constable :**
He’s got ironwork under the straw, that Mathurin. Two great demobilisation trunks, reinforced with wrought iron that caught the light in the shadows. He must have salvaged the lot from a State wagon, that’s certain — somewhere near the Pretty Girls’ halt. But to see all that through the gaps in the boards, I had to do a bit of bargaining. I made him a little deal: a sham official quarantine to protect his still and leave him alone.

**The Cripple :** *(authoritatively)*
What do you mean, an order? Who gave you permission to—

**The Village Constable :** *(taking half a step back)*
But he threatened me with a pitchfork as long as a sabre!

**The Cripple :**
Enough! We’ll come back to that. But are you quite sure of what you saw? Military trunks?

**The Village Constable :**
As sure as I’m standing here, Major. Army chests, heavy as sin and well hidden. That’s exactly why I sold him that ridiculous quarantine notice: to keep him quiet long enough for us to see what’s inside.

**The Cripple :** *(a silence; his gaze drifts away for a moment before returning to the constable)*
All right. But not a word to anyone. Go and make your rounds at the bottom of the village, and keep that bloody mouth shut.

*(The Village Constable gives a brisk nod and leaves, closing the door behind him.)*

**The Cripple :** *(rising slowly, leaning on his stick, his eyes naked with greed)*
You’ll never believe it, Midwife… At last! At last I’ve found my ancestors’ trunk! It was right under my nose, while I’ve been searching through the old-country papers to find it! That Mathurin has been sitting on this secret for eighty years, under his hay… There it is — my trunk, waiting for me since the last century. It’s in that barn.

*(He breaks off, his eyes feverish, and cries out:)*

**The Cripple :** My treasure!

*(The Midwife, caught off guard, gives a tender little smile and takes a step towards him, believing for one second that he is finally speaking to her. Without even seeing her, the Cripple barrels on, pointing towards the barn.)*

**The Cripple :**
The one… the one my family paid for in blood, damn it!

**The Midwife :** *(her smile instantly freezing, disappointment swallowed in a violent clench of the jaw)*
Ah. Your trunk. Of course.

*(A pause.)*

**The Midwife :**
Major! I’m telling you again. Give me my pass to Mathurin’s farm. She won’t last another few days. I have to go!

**The Cripple :**
No! She’ll just have to wait! I couldn’t care less about Mathurin’s wife. I need that chest. The Constable must seal it at once, before anyone moves a single bundle of hay!

**The Midwife :**
But surely you realise, Major… Mathurin’s family is already starving. They’ve only got a little girl, barely six years old, to help with the animals. No able-bodied man since the Somme, and the mother is bedridden, fainting away. If you shut them in with your quarantine, I’m warning you: Mathurin will burn his own barn to the ground rather than let you lay a hand on his hay.

The Cripple, dark-faced and stubborn, turns his back without another word and disappears towards the back room. The Midwife, fists clenched in barely contained fury, spins sharply on her heels and heads for the door.

Just as she crosses the threshold, she stops dead.

In the doorway to the inner room stands the carpenter’s wife. She carries herself with provocative confidence and slips her hand into the Cripple’s. He does not pull away. Instead, he welcomes her with a knowing, conspiratorial smile.

A deathly silence falls over the room.

The Midwife stares at them, rooted to the spot, her eyes wide with a mute, black fury twisting her insides. The ground seems to give way beneath her feet. She takes half a step back, choking on hatred, then tears herself away in a flash.
