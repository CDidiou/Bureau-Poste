*(The temporary post office. The place is saturated with stamps, municipal registers and official telegrams. The Major is installed behind the large wooden counter.
On this improvised desk, the eternal patinated bronze bust of Balthasar d'André, known as L'Epicier. The inscription states Honorary Citizen, illustrious child of the village. President of the Constituent Assembly. The major, with a sovereign air, sorts the passes and the requisition slips. The Garde Champêtre stands in front of him, kepi in hand. The Midwife enters, her shawl on her shoulders, her eyes sparkling.)*

**The Midwife :** *(approaching the desk, voice lower)*
My major... I need a pass for the Mathers farm. I have a check-up before the birth. I'm worried.

**The Lame Man :** *(without looking up from his tampons)*
I know it. But I can't sign anything. The area is under the order.

**The Midwife :** *(a slightly forced smile, trying to catch her eye)*
Women carry life, and their pains do not care about administrative hesitations. I need this pass urgently.

**The Lame Man :** *(turning abruptly towards the Garde Champêtre)*
And you, the Guard? Did you do what I told you?

**The Garde Champêtre:** *(approaching in a low voice, his eyes glinting with mischief)*
I'll come back, Major. He blocked his door with heavy blows of planks, but I have the eye, you know!

**The Lame Man :** *(standing still, his gaze suddenly sharp)*
And?

**The Village Constable :**
He has scrap metal under the straw, Mathers. Two large demobilization trunks, with wrought iron reinforcements that gleamed in the shadows. He did his job on a state wagon, that's for sure, near the Belles-Filles stopover. But to see all that through the cracks in the boards, I had to negotiate. I gave him a little arrangement: a fake official quarantine to protect his still and leave him alone.

**The Lame Man :** *(authoritarian)*
What do you mean, a decree? Who allowed you to...

**The Village Constable :** *(stepping back half a step)*
But he threatened me with a pitchfork as long as a cutlass!

**The Lame Man :**
Okay, enough! We'll talk about it again. But are you sure of what you saw? Military trunks?

**The Village Constable :**
As sure as I am speaking to you, Major. Army chests, very heavy and well hidden. That's why I sold him this charade of a poster: time to keep him quiet and see what we find there.

**The Lame Man :** *(a silence, his gaze wandering for a moment before returning to the guard)*
Okay. But no more words to anyone. Go do your rounds at the bottom of the village, and keep that damn mouth shut.
*(The Garde Champêtre promptly salutes and leaves, closing the door behind him.)*

**The Lame Man :** *(rising slowly, leaning on his staff, his eyes raw with lust)*
You will never believe it, Midwife... Finally! Finally I find it, this trunk of my ancestors! She was before my eyes, I who searched even the papers of the old continent to look for her! This Mathers who has been harboring this secret for eighty years under his hay... There it is, my trunk which has been waiting for me since the other century. She's in this barn.
*(He interrupts, looking hallucinated, and says :)*
My treasure!
*(The Midwife, taken by surprise, gives a tender smile and takes a step towards him, believing for a second that he is finally speaking to her. The Lame Man, without even seeing her, moves on in one go, pointing to the barn.)*
The one... the one my family paid for with their blood, damn it! 

**The Midwife :** *(the smile instantly freezes, swallowing her disappointment in a violent clenching of the jaw)*
Oh. Your trunk. Of course. 
(Pause)
Major! I repeat it to you. Give me my pass for the Mathers farm. She won't last a few more days, I have to go!

**The Lame Man :**
It's no! She just has to wait! I don't care about Mathers's wife. I need this chest, the Guard must immediately place his seal on it before we move a single bale of hay!

**The Midwife :**
But realize, Major... The family of that guy Mathers is already dying of poverty. They only have a girl of barely six years old to help them with the animals. No more able-bodied men since the Somme, and the bedridden mother who is spinning. If you close the door on them with your quarantine, I warn you: Mathers will set fire to his own barn rather than let you touch his hay.*(The Lame, with a dark and stubborn look, turns his back without another word, heading towards the back room. The Midwife, her fists clenched in suppressed rage, turns with a sharp movement of her heels to leave. As she crosses the threshold, she stops abruptly. On the threshold of the interior door, the carpenter's wife appears. She struts with provocative confidence, and comes sliding her hand in that of the Lame Man. He does not push her away: he welcomes her with a knowing, knowing smile. A dead silence falls in the room. The Midwife looks at them, frozen in place, her eyes bulging with a silent, black fury that twists her insides. She takes a half step back, suffocating with hatred.)*
