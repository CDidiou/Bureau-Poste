*(The dimness of a small attic room beneath the roof tiles. The silence is heavy, thick, broken only by the scratch of a quill upon worn paper. On the rough wooden table, the candle is burning down into a pool of wax. In one corner of the room stands the great wooden chest, closed, covered with a fine layer of dust. Zephyrine Pearl, stooped now, her face hollowed by wrinkles and solitude, draws one final flourish.)*

*(She blows gently upon the manuscript, its ink still fresh, then carefully lays her quill beside the inkwell.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :** *(in a barely audible whisper, stroking with her fingertips the page whose edges seem to fade into darkness)*
It is written… It is all here. Not a single thorn, not a bitter tear, not a hurtful glance.

*(She turns towards the great wooden chest. She opens it. Inside, she places the bound pages. Then she slowly closes the chest.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :**
The influenza took your breath, fear twisted your heart, and flight drove you apart. The world judged you too small, too cowardly, too wretched to survive the night… and you, Philip, chose instead to throw yourself into the arms of that creature. That married woman, that loose woman whose morals were the talk of the whole village, whose bastard child was not even her husband’s blood! Doubly vile, triply impious — and for her, you turned your eyes away from mine!

In these pages, Philip, you will be cursed by your wife. You will be broken like a man without a home, without warmth, and you will bitterly dream of a true family, the one I could have given you — I, the humble nurse who watched over bodies while others defiled them. The centuries will see your heart grown hard, your hands greedy for acres of land, houses and timber!

*(She slowly closes her eyes. A single gentle tear slips down her face, furrowed by age, catching one last glimmer of candlelight.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :** *(in a final breath filled with immense peace)*
Philip, in your posthumous confession, you will understand what you should have understood while you were alive, there beneath my eyes, on the cursed day when I caught you. And for this hatred that consumed me for half a century, for the poison you poured into my soul, I forgive you, my Philip. I love you. I loved you.

*(Her head gently falls against her shoulder. Her limp hand slips from the edge of the table. The candle dies in a thin plume of blue smoke. Absolute silence descends once more.)*

---

*(Three days later. The harsh, cold morning light streams through the attic skylight. The sounds of children quarrelling carry up the stairs. The door is struck by a sharp kick and flies open. The carpenter’s little boy bounds in, a broom handle clutched in his hand. His elder sister, wary, remains on the threshold. The boy creeps towards the old woman sitting motionless in her chair, her head resting beside the closed chest.)*

**The Carpenter’s Grandson :** *(approaching cautiously, pointing at her with the end of his stick, then letting out a piercing cry that echoes beneath the roof tiles)*
The old woman’s finally dead! Dad! Dad, come quick! You’ll be pleased!

*(The children turn and run down the stairs, laughing, leaving the lifeless body of the Midwife lying beside the chest.)*
