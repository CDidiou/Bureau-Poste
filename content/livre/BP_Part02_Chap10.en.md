*(The darkness of a small attic room under the tiles. The silence there is heavy, thick, interrupted only by the crunch of a goose feather on the worn paper. On the rough wooden table, the candle finishes burning in a puddle of wax. In a corner of the room rests the large wooden chest, closed, covered with a fine layer of dust. Zephyrine Pearl, hunched over, her face hollowed out by wrinkles and solitude, draws a final loop. She gently blows on the still fresh ink of the manuscript, then delicately places her quill next to the inkwell.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :**
*(in a barely audible whisper, stroking the night-bordered leaf with his fingertips)*
It's written... It's all there. Not a bramble, not a bitter tear, not a look that hurts.

*(She turns towards the large wooden chest. She opens it. She places bound pages in it. She slowly closes the chest.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :**
The flu took your breath, fear twisted your heart, and flight separated you. The world judged you too small, too cowardly, too miserable to survive the night... and you, Philip, preferred to throw yourself into the arms of this creature. This married woman, this promiscuous whore about whom the whole village was gossiping, and whose bastard was not even of her husband's blood! Doubly infamous, triply impious, and it is for her that your eyes were turned away from mine!

In these pages, Philip, you will be cursed by your wife, you will be broken like a man without home, without warmth, and you will dream bitterly of a real family, the one that I could offer you, me, the simple nurse who watched over the bodies when others defiled them. The centuries will see your dry heart, your hands greedy for acres of land, houses, and frames!

*(She slowly closes her eyes. A soft tear slides down her age-painted cheek, catching a last reflection of the candle.)*

**Zephyrine Pearl :**
*(in a last breath imbued with immense peace)*
Philip, you will understand in your posthumous confession what you should have understood during your lifetime, there, before my eyes, on the cursed day when I surprised you. And for this hatred that consumed me for half a century, for this poison that you poured into my soul, I forgive you, my Philip. I love you. I loved you.

*(His head falls gently on his shoulder. His relaxed hand slips from the edge of the table. The candle goes out in a fine blue smoke. Silence falls again, absolute.)*

---

*(Three days later. The harsh, cold morning light shines through the skylight of the attic. Sounds of children squabbling. The door sounds with a sharp kick and flies open. The carpenter's little one comes bounding in, holding a broomstick in his hand. His big sister, cautious, stays in the doorway. The little one approaches gently the old woman motionless in her chair, her head leaning next to the chest closed.)*

**The carpenter's grandson:** *(with careful steps, pointing at her with the end of his wood, then uttering a loud piercing cry which resonates under the tiles)*
The old woman is finally dead! Dad ! Dad, come quickly! You will be happy!

*(The kids run and laugh back down the stairs, leaving the midwife's lifeless body lying next to the chest.)*
