*(Night has fallen. The meeting has just broken up. Beneath the market hall, only the Midwife, the Cripple, the Priest and the Village Constable remain. The Constable is wiping his drum with his sleeve.)*

**The Village Constable :** *(grumbling)*
Twenty francs' worth of cresyl... They expect us to douse the town hall's earthen floor with twenty francs' worth of disinfectant! It'll stink of the doctor's surgery for ten miles around.

**The Midwife :**
It's not the floor they're worried about, for heaven's sake. It's people's lungs. And your cresyl won't stop women giving birth. Mélie, Mathurin's cousin, is due on Sunday, and his own wife is due in a fortnight, provided there are no further complications. If you block the roads with your cordons sanitaires, how am I supposed to get through?

**The Cripple :** *(leaning against a pillar, faintly amused)*
You'll go on foot, my dear. The Prefect was quite clear: no one crosses the boundary without the white slip of paper and the post office stamp.

**The Midwife :**
The bloody stamp! To hell with their stamps! A child on the way isn't going to wait for the postman to finish his drop of brandy!

**The Priest :** *(calmly, his hands clasped behind his back)*
The postman won't be drinking much more, Mademoiselle. The district post office is being requisitioned first thing tomorrow morning. They are installing a priority telegraph service there for the Army and the Prefecture. Private letters will come after the health reports.

**The Cripple :** *(his eyes lighting up as he rapidly calculates)*
The post office... In that derelict old house! On the corner of Rue de la Gousse-Poivrée? Why, it's a sieve! The roof has leaked ever since fourteen, and the lease register was never properly brought up to date after the troops were billeted there.
And if the Administration moves in without checking the deeds, they'll end up approving an eighteen-ninety lease that says the ground floor belongs to the Church and the roof to the heirs! They'll spend their first six months in office handing out letters while eating their lunches on the doorstep, because they won't know which saint to pray to before they can even open the door.

**The Priest :** *(watching the Cripple from the corner of his eye)*
The Republic doesn't ask the land registry for permission when fever is on the rise, Major. It takes whatever is still standing.

**The Cripple :**
With this new post office, Father, official forms won't be passing through the parish records anymore: death certificates, requisitions for straw, arrangements for transporting the dead. And someone will have to fill in the columns, explain to the peasants what they're signing, and make sure the Prefecture doesn't count ten beds where we've got four. Those city bureaucrats couldn't decipher a local name without getting it wrong.

**The Village Constable :** *(suspiciously)*
And you're volunteering, are you, Cripple? To handle the blotting paper and the ink bottle?

**The Cripple :** *(with a small sideways smile, adjusting his crutch)*
Someone has to make sure the paperwork stays straight while you splash the stones with your English dishwater. We can't leave the district to the gentlemen from the city.

**The Midwife :**
Do you think I'll be able to get through on Sunday for Mélie? And what about Mathurin's wife? Will that be possible too?

**The Cripple :** *(his voice softening slightly)*
What I think, Mademoiselle, is that if the post office sets up there, the supplies will come down the lower road. And your linen bandages with them. Tomorrow morning I'll be at the barn, putting up the first trestle tables for your emergency hospital. We'll need a workbench too. It'll come in handy for getting organised.
Yes. A workbench! You always need a workbench.
