*(It is dark. The meeting has just dispersed. The midwife, the lame man, the priest and the country guard who is wiping his drum with his sleeve remain in the hall.)*

**The Village Constable :** *(groaning)*
Twenty francs of cresyl... They want us to water the dirt floor of the town hall with twenty francs of disinfectant! It's going to stink up the doctor for ten miles around.

**The Midwife :**
It's not the earth that worries them, it's the lungs. And your cresyl will not prevent women from giving birth. La Mélie, Mathers's cousin, is due on Sunday, and his own wife is due within a fortnight, if there are no more complications for the latter. If you block the paths with your sanitary cordons, how will I be able to get through?

**The Lame Man :** *(leaning against a pillar, a bit mockingly)*
You will pass on foot, my dear friend. The prefect was categorical: no one crosses the limit without the rectangle of white paper and the postmark.

**The Midwife :**
The stamp! The plague be on their tampons! A child who pushes doesn't wait until the postman has finished drinking his drop!

**The Priest :** *(calm, hands clasped behind the back)*
The postman won't drink much anymore, Mademoiselle. The canton's post office building will be requisitioned tomorrow morning. The priority telegraph for the army and the prefecture was installed there. Private letters will come after health dispatches.

**The Lame Man :** *(his gaze comes alive, calculating quickly)*
The post office... In this dilapidated house! At the corner of rue de la Gousse-Poivrée? But it’s a real sieve! The roof has been leaking for fourteen years, and the lease register has never been purged with the cantonment. And if the Administration moves there without checking further, they will validate a lease from 1890 which stipulates that the ground floor belongs to the church and the roof to the heirs! They will spend their first semester in office distributing letters while breaking bread on the steps, not knowing which saint to turn to to open the door.

**The Priest :** *(observing the Lame Man out of the corner of his eye)*
The Republic does not ask permission from the land registry when the fever rises, Major. She takes what is standing.

**The Lame Man :**
With this new post office, father, official forms will no longer go through the parish registers: deaths, straw requisitions, transport of bodies. And we will need someone to fill in the columns, explain to the farmers what they are signing, and check that the prefecture does not count us ten beds where we have four. These city bureaucrats can't decipher a name from here without making a mistake.

**The Village Constable :** *(suspicious)*
And you offer yourself, the lame one? To hold the blotter and the ink bottle?

**The Lame Man :** *(a little smirk, adjusting his crutch)*
Someone has to make sure the papers stay straight while you hose down the stones with your English dishwashing water. We are not abandoning the canton to the gentlemen of the city.

**The Midwife :**
Do you think I will be able to come to La Mélie on Sunday? And for Mathers's wife, will this be possible too?

**The Lame Man :** *(his tone softening slightly)*
Above all, I think, Mademoiselle, that if the post office is set up there, help will arrive via the lower road. And your linen strips with it. Tomorrow morning, I will be at the barn to set up the first trestles for your emergency hospital. We will also need a workbench, this will be useful for getting organized. Yes, a workbench! A workbench is always necessary.
