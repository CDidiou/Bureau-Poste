*(At the presbytery. The priest is sitting near the window, ironing a small factory register with a cloth. The midwife is wiping the kitchen table. There is a sharp knock twice against the doorpost.)*

**The Lame Man:**
Is it open for disabled people or do you have to come back on market day?

**The Priest:**
Sit down, Major, instead of standing there wearing out your good leg.

**The Lame Man:**
Thank you very much
*(He sits down, places his hat on his knees. His eyes linger for a moment on the hands of the Midwife who are putting away the vials.)*
I hadn't seen you, Madam. Finally... Miss. All my respects. You take good care of our brave priest.

**The Midwife:**
Hello, Mr. Major. The care of souls and bodies does not take a vacation.

**The Priest:***(without looking up from his register, in a calm voice)*
I was reading while waiting for you. The silence here helps with reflection.

**The Lame Man:**
Still immersed in the Holy Books?

**The Priest:***(turning a page)*
Not only that. The diocese has just sent me the pre-war land register statements for the canton. Those of the bishopric. They are sometimes more talkative than those at the town hall.

**The Lame Man:** *(straightening up slightly)*
Oh? And what beautiful things do they say about the bishop's papers?

**The Priest:** *(in an almost detached voice, ironing his cloth over the leather of the register)*
They say that since at least 1837, the Roche-Fontaine plot has had no right of way over the municipal area. But above all... that a marginal note from the factory mentions a cache. A document from the bishopric speaks of three sealed cassettes, buried under the corner marker. A plum tree.

**The Lame Man:** *(a half-smile at the corner of his lips)*
1837... It was the year of the patriots and the insurrections in Lower Canada. The notaries of the July Monarchy wrote with a quill pen and confused the pistoles and the Louis d'or. A comma misplaced on a plan, Monsieur le Curé, and the gold of my ancestors slides a meter towards the north.

**The Priest:***(amused, without looking up)*
Your ancestors had memories, Major. But the archives of the diocese have a longer view than your shovels.

**The Midwife:** *(placing a cup of steaming infusion in front of the Major)*
So leave the notaries alone and drink this. And stop telling the inn that you're moving the terminals with a wooden leg. The constable is stupid, but he has a two-meter tongue.

**The Lame Man:** *(he takes the cup. Their fingers brush against each other for a moment; he stills his gesture, a little uncertain)*
Let's see... I can't say things that didn't happen. You see, I am in the middle of transplanting new fruit trees, new stems. The village is in great need of fruit, you will agree.

**The Priest:***(in a detached voice, continuing to iron his cloth)*
The town notary told me that an educated man can make a deed of sale say many things. So many good people still don't know how to decipher contracts and their clauses...

**The Lame Man:** *(staring at the Priest with a cold look)*
You are absolutely right, Father. Education must do its work, and much faster than my fruit trees. For my part, I don't pick a fight with anyone. Besides, here are the accounts.
*(He pulls a folded paper from under his arm and places it on the table)*
This is the list of herbal teas for women in the lower canton. I recorded everything as you wanted. Everything is clear.

**The Priest:**
So be it, Major. Heaven will keep the accounts.

**The Midwife:**
We're missing so many ingredients for poultices... You can't imagine. The needs are so great in the canton. You are precious to us, Major. To all of us.

*(The Lame looks at her for a moment. A silence settles, heavy with implicitness, under the vigilant gaze of the priest.)*

**The Lame Man:** *(low, getting up with difficulty)*
Enough thanks. I saw some suffering. More than an entire existence should bear. And then... if there was no one to decipher the labels on your vials, you'd end up poisoning the constable for good.

**The Priest:** *(gently closing his register)*
That would be a great shame. The town would lose its only night eye. Have a nice day, Major.

**The Lame Man:**
Mr. Priest... Mademoiselle.

*(He bows slightly towards the Midwife, places his hat on his head and leaves, making the regular impact of his wooden leg resonate on the cement of the entrance.)*
